## Background

I am a medical graduate who transitioned into robotics research, pursuing an MRes in Medical Robotics and Image-Guided Intervention at Imperial College London's Hamlyn Centre for Medical Robotics.

My work combines clinical problem-solving with hands-on engineering, spanning mechanical design, CAD, rapid prototyping, embedded systems, kinematic modelling, and experimental validation.

## Research Interests

- Medical and surgical robotics
- Continuum and deployable robotics
- Mechanical design and biomimetic mechanisms
- Search-and-rescue and field robotics
- Mechatronic systems and robotic actuation

## Featured Research

My MRes thesis, *Deployable Hybrid Continuum-Eversion Robot for Medical Applications in Disaster Scenarios*, explored a robotic platform designed to deploy into confined environments while retaining the ability to steer and deliver a localized intervention.

The project involved developing a deployable eversion mechanism, a tendon-driven rolling-contact-joint steering module, a continuous central working channel, and a vortex-cannon end-effector. I worked across mechanical design, fabrication, integration, kinematic modelling, and physical testing.

## Engineering Approach

I am particularly interested in translating mechanical concepts into working hardware. My approach combines analytical modelling with iterative prototyping and experimental evaluation, with an emphasis on understanding how design decisions affect real-world performance.

Coming from a medical background rather than a conventional engineering degree, I have developed my engineering knowledge through self-directed learning and practical research.

## Technical Skills

**Mechanical design:** CAD, SolidWorks, mechanical assemblies, 3D printing and prototyping.

**Robotics:** Continuum mechanisms, tendon-driven actuation, deployable systems and kinematic modelling.

**Electronics and control:** Arduino, stepper motors, motor drivers and hardware integration.

**Simulation and documentation:** MATLAB, technical writing, LaTeX and experimental analysis.
